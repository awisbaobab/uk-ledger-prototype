How incidents are scored · tap to open

A log of UK cases reported in the news in which asylum seekers and people who entered illegally (status as stated by the sources) were convicted of, or charged with, a crime. It is not a measure of crime rates and is not representative of asylum seekers or migrants as a whole. Most entries are convictions.

Pending cases appear once a person is charged, or briefly when at least three independent X accounts (no bots or campaign accounts) report the same incident with matching place, date and type. X-only cards are labelled "Reported on X, unverified", score no more than 10%, name nobody, and are removed after 3 days unless police, courts or media confirm them. All pending cards are marked "Not convicted" and use only the wording of police, CPS and court statements, in line with UK contempt of court rules. No one is named before charge.

Verification score, in four stages dated by the offence: Posts (social media only, up to 10%), Media (reputable outlets, 40–70%), Police (arrest 80%, statement 90%, charge 95%), Court (conviction, 100%). A police denial, dropped case or acquittal lowers the score and is labelled rather than deleted.

Immigration-status flag, separate from the score and recorded only as stated by the court or credible sources: Status: verified when police, the CPS, a court or judge (including what was said in court as reported by mainstream outlets) or the Home Office states the person is an asylum seeker or entered the UK illegally; Status: pending verification when it rests only on media or other unofficial sources. The basis is shown next to the flag. Wording follows court and source language.

The header shows the position of the card in view and how many convictions are dated up to it; pending cases count as cases, not convictions. Corrections are welcome.
